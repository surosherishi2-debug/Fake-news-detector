# 2. Related Work

The field of automated misinformation mitigation encompasses three interdependent subdisciplines: claim detection and check-worthiness estimation, evidence retrieval and entailment verification, and sociotechnical consensus modeling.

## 2.1 Claim Decomposition and Check-Worthiness Estimation
Before verifying an unstructured document, automated systems must disentangle verifiable empirical assertions from subjective opinions, hyperbole, and conversational framing. The ClaimBuster framework \cite{hassan2017claimbuster} formalized check-worthiness estimation by training supervised sentence classifiers on transcripts of political debates, identifying sentences containing quantifiable assertions, legislative actions, or historical claims. Subsequent shared evaluation campaigns, notably the CLEF CheckThat! Lab (2020--2024) \cite{nakov2024checkthat}, expanded check-worthiness estimation across multigenre content and multilingual corpora in over 15 languages. Modern verification architectures build upon this paradigm by decomposing compound news articles into sets of atomic propositions $C = \{c_1, c_2, \dots, c_k\}$, enabling sentence-level justification and localized error attribution rather than monolithic document-level classification.

## 2.2 Evidence-Conditioned Fact Verification
Early approaches to automated fake news detection treated veracity assessment as a closed-world text classification task, training models on surface n-grams or fine-tuned Transformer representations. However, multiple studies demonstrate that text-only classifiers overfit to stylistic artifacts and entity frequency biases rather than verifying truth. 

To resolve this limitation, Thorne et al. \cite{thorne2018fever} introduced the FEVER benchmark (Fact Extraction and VERification), casting fact-checking as a three-way textual entailment problem: given a factual claim and candidate evidence documents, classify the relationship as **SUPPORTS**, **REFUTES**, or **NOT ENOUGH INFO**. Extending this principle to political discourse, Alhindi et al. \cite{alhindi2018liarplus} introduced LIAR-Plus by extracting human-written justification summaries from PolitiFact reports, establishing that conditioning veracity models on explicit evidence sentences significantly improves macro-F1 and robustness compared to isolated statement classification. Similarly, in the biomedical and healthcare domain, Cui and Lee \cite{cui2020coaid} established the CoAID benchmark, pairing COVID-19 health claims with authoritative fact-checking debunk articles.

## 2.3 Temporal Obsolescence in Dynamic Information Ecosystems
A fundamental weakness of static fact-checking models is temporal obsolescence. Factual veracity is inherently time-conditioned: historical debunks may not apply to evolving breaking news, while established scientific consensus exhibits significant stability over multi-decade intervals. Recent evaluation campaigns, such as TSVer (2025) and Quantemp (2024), demonstrate that static benchmark evaluations suffer from temporal leakage, where models fail when evaluating claims outside their training temporal window. By formalizing temporal evidence decay as an exponential attenuation function $w(t) = \exp(-\lambda \Delta t)$ with domain-adaptive parameters ($\lambda_{\text{politics}} > \lambda_{\text{health}} > \lambda_{\text{science}}$), our architecture systematically balances historical consensus against contemporary updates.

## 2.4 Adversarial Robustness and Neural Vulnerability
Deep neural language models are notoriously brittle under adversarial perturbation. Jin et al. \cite{jin2020textfooler} demonstrated with TextFooler that state-of-the-art BERT models experience dramatic accuracy collapses under greedy synonym substitution in counter-fitted embedding space, while preserving human semantic comprehension. Morris et al. \cite{morris2020textattack} unified these evaluation protocols within the modular TextAttack framework. In automated misinformation detection, adversarial robustness is critical: bad actors actively rewrite deceptive headlines using synonym substitutions and syntactic paraphrases to evade keyword and text-only classifiers. Grounding classifications upon external accredited evidence networks shields systems from purely lexical perturbations.

## 2.5 Decentralized Bipartisan Consensus and Sociotechnical Defense
Centralized automated fact-checking systems frequently encounter accusations of institutional bias, leading to user distrust across polarized political clusters. To overcome this dilemma, decentralized consensus mechanisms model annotator polarity and note helpfulness. The X/Twitter Community Notes platform implements open-source regularized matrix factorization over user rating matrices, identifying bridging notes that achieve broad consensus across raters with opposing latent ideological coordinates. TruthLens-Omni incorporates this formulation directly within its epistemic verification layer, ensuring that consensus status is granted only to notes supported by ideologically diverse observers.

## 2.6 Multilingual & Indic Language Transfer
While the vast majority of fact-checking literature focuses on English, misinformation spreads rapidly across regional vernacular languages. Khanuja et al. \cite{khanuja2021muril} introduced MuRIL (Multilingual Representations for Indian Languages), demonstrating that pre-training on 17 Indian languages and transliterated scripts dramatically outperforms generic multilingual models on downstream Indic classification tasks. Conneau et al. \cite{conneau2020xlmr} established the cross-lingual transfer capabilities of XLM-RoBERTa. Our framework evaluates both cross-lingual zero-shot and few-shot transfer across English, Hindi, and Marathi to provide genuine regional coverage.
