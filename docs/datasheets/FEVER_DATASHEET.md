# Datasheet for FEVER Benchmark Dataset (Stratified Verification Subset)

**Authors:** James Thorne, Andreas Vlachos, Christos Christodoulopoulos, Arpit Mittal  
**Publication:** *FEVER: a Large-scale Dataset for Fact Extraction and VERification* (NAACL-HLT 2018)  
**Standard:** Gebru et al. (2021) *Datasheets for Datasets*  
**Date of Curation:** 2026-11-27  

---

## 1. Motivation
- **Purpose:** Developed to benchmark automated fact extraction and claim verification systems against encyclopedic evidence.
- **Problem Solved:** Overcomes monolithic classification by coupling factual claims directly with sentence-level Wikipedia evidence, introducing an explicit class for ungrounded claims (`NOT ENOUGH INFO`).

---

## 2. Composition
- **Total Samples:** 10,000 stratified samples curated from `fever.ai/download/fever/train.jsonl`.
- **Stratified Class Balance:**
  - `SUPPORTS` $\to$ **`VERIFIED`** (~3,333 samples)
  - `REFUTES` $\to$ **`REFUTED`** (~3,333 samples)
  - `NOT ENOUGH INFO` $\to$ **`DATA_VOID`** (~3,334 samples)
- **Key Feature for TruthLens:** The `NOT ENOUGH INFO` class directly trains and calibrates TruthLens's **Zero-Assumption Principle**, ensuring the system learns to abstain rather than hallucinate confidence.

---

## 3. Collection & Preprocessing
- **Source:** English Wikipedia introductory sections.
- **Collection Method:** Human annotators generated claims by altering Wikipedia sentences (negation, substitution, generalization) and finding supporting/refuting sentences.
- **Preprocessing:** Filtering non-ascii anomalies, stripping invalid JSON lines, deduplication on normalized claim text.

---

## 4. Known Biases & Limitations
- **Synthetic Claim Phrasing:** Because claims were generated by human annotators prompted to mutate Wikipedia sentences, linguistic phrasing can occasionally exhibit crowdsourcing artifacts (e.g. repetitive negation patterns).
- **Domain Skew:** Predominantly encyclopedic topics (celebrities, historical events, geography, pop culture).
